# Medical and Scientific Slide Checks

Load this reference for clinical, biomedical, or other evidence-sensitive presentations. It supplements the general deck QA; it does not replace professional review.

## Freeze the Evidence Version

Record the exact source and version used for each claim: guideline edition, paper/report, registry or trial identifier, DOI or stable URL, publication date, data cutoff, and follow-up duration when available. Keep primary-source findings distinct from secondary summaries, recommendations, and the author's interpretation.

## Trial and Outcome Fields

Check the slide and fact ledger for the fields that apply:

- Disease, stage/subtype, eligibility criteria, prior treatment, treatment line, and relevant biomarker definition.
- Study phase and design, randomization ratio, analysis population, arm names, sample sizes, regimen, dose, schedule, and comparator.
- Endpoint name and assessment method; event count, median or rate, effect estimate, confidence interval, P value, timepoint, and data cutoff.
- Safety denominator, grading system, severity threshold, attribution, and whether the event is all-cause or treatment-related.
- Crossover, immature follow-up, missing data, multiplicity, subgroup status, and other limitations that change interpretation.

## Wording Guardrails

- Preserve qualifiers such as “not statistically significant,” “not yet mature,” “not established,” “exploratory,” and “numerically higher.” Do not convert them into positive or negative certainty.
- Keep efficacy, safety, and surrogate endpoints distinct. Do not infer overall-survival benefit from progression-free-survival results.
- Do not generalize subgroup findings or indirect comparisons into direct comparative claims.
- Keep units, decimal precision, denominators, inequality signs, confidence-interval bounds, and statistical notation unchanged unless the source supports a correction.
- Put a traceable source note on the relevant slide; ensure it is readable in the rendered deck.

## Visual Integrity

- Treat generated illustrations as illustrative, not anatomical, diagnostic, or trial evidence unless independently verified and labeled.
- Do not use a decorative bar or icon as if it were a Kaplan-Meier curve, proportional effect, or validated scale. Label schematic visuals as schematic.
- Recheck every value and claim after image-to-slide reconstruction; OCR and image models can transpose digits, alter symbols, and drop qualifiers.
